The criterion looked rigorous: the detector would have to show, with 95% confidence, that it detects at least 90% of the faults, over 29 runs per scenario. I did the math before running and saw that this criterion only passes with 29 of 29, and that a detector with 97.5% detection, the rate the simulation predicted, would pass 48% of the time. In practice it was a coin flip.

This is a short post, with one figure per idea instead of the blog's full set: the topic is a calculation. The context is the [gray-failure canary experiment]({{ "/en/2026/10/28/gray-failure-canary-istio/" | relative_url }}).

## Summary

- **With 29 runs, "lower bound ≥ 0.90" requires 29 of 29.** With 28 of 29, the one-sided Clopper-Pearson lower bound [1] drops below 0.90.
- **Requiring perfection makes the result hostage to a single run.** At a true rate of 97.5%, the chance of 29 hits in a row is 0.975²⁹ ≈ 48%.
- **Keeping the bound at 0.90 would be expensive:** an 80% chance of passing would take about 70 runs per scenario, more than double.
- **The switch:** 30 runs and a lower bound ≥ 0.80, which tolerates up to 2 misses. Chance of passing: 96% at a 97.5% true rate, 81% at 95%. Chance of passing a bad detector (70%): 0.2%.
- **The 90% target stays in the report**, as a descriptive point estimate, just not as the pass criterion.

## The math

The criterion uses the one-sided 95% lower bound of the Clopper-Pearson interval, which is exact for proportions. For each number of runs you can list how many misses the criterion tolerates and the chance of passing given a true rate. The formula is the binomial sum from 0 up to the number of tolerated misses.

| Criterion | Runs | Misses tolerated | Passes if the true rate is 97.5% | 95% | 70% (bad detector) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v4: lower bound ≥ 0.90 | 29 | 0 | 48% | 23% | 0.0% |
| v5: lower bound ≥ 0.80 | 30 | 2 | 96% | 81% | 0.2% |

How to read it: both criteria almost never pass a bad detector. The difference is how often each one fails a good detector.

## What if I wanted to keep 0.90?

<figure class="fig">
<img src="{{ "/assets/img/criterio-29-de-29/execucoes-necessarias-en.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Chance of passing at a 97.5% true rate and bound ≥ 0.90, by number of runs: 48% with 29, 36% with 40, 68% with 46, 56% with 60, 78% with 64, 88% with 76, 92% with 90. Misses tolerated above each point: 0, 0, 0, 1, 1, 1, 2, 3, 4." loading="lazy">
<figcaption>Keeping the bound at 0.90, the chance of passing climbs in steps: each time the criterion starts tolerating one more miss, it jumps and then falls until the next step. 80% shows up around 70 runs.</figcaption>
</figure>

The curve has teeth. With the same number of tolerated misses, more runs raise the chance of one more miss showing up, and the chance of passing drops. Only when the criterion starts tolerating an extra miss does it jump. That is why 40 runs would be worse than 29.

Each fault run takes about 16 minutes in the cluster. Going from 30 to 70 per scenario, across four scenarios, would add about 43 hours of runs, or 21 more hours of wall-clock time with both clusters in parallel. The choice was to lower the bound to 0.80, which still separates a good detector from a bad one, and keep 30 runs.

## When the switch was made

The switch went into v5 of the [pre-registration]({{ "/en/2026/11/08/pre-registro-experimento-kubernetes/" | relative_url }}), before any evaluation data, driven by the math above and not by a result. The evaluation then gave 30 of 30 in both main scenarios (lower bound 0.905). The old criterion would also have passed that result. But had a single run failed, the old one would have failed a detector with 97% accuracy.

## Limits

- The 97.5% rate comes from the simulation, and the simulation got the absolute latency level wrong (it got the decisions right). If the true rate were lower, the new criterion would have less power (81% at 95%).
- Lowering the bound from 0.90 to 0.80 weakens the claim: "at least 80%" is less than "at least 90%". The report shows the point estimate and the bound obtained, so readers can judge.

## How to reproduce

In [`labs/gray-failure-canary-istio/`](https://github.com/mariomenezes/mariomenezes.github.io/tree/main/labs/gray-failure-canary-istio): `python3 scripts/extrair.py` recomputes the curves (the `power` section of `data/figuras.json`) with the same `clopper_pearson` used in the analysis (`code/triage/calib_lib.py`). Criterion: 0.480 for v4 and 0.962 for v5, at 0.975. One line of Python checks the main point: `0.975**29` gives 0.48.
